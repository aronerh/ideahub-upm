# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users

Primary: UPM students who have an idea and want it published quickly, usually from a laptop or phone, without
preparing anything first. The design serves them first.

Secondary: investors and other visitors who browse published ideas without an account.

## Product Purpose

IdeaHub UPM is where UPM students publish their ideas and investors find them. It was built for the Agile
Methods course (EMSE, UPM, 2026-27, Team 4). The professor, Ana Moreno, is the Product Owner and accepts or
rejects each user story in the sprint demos.

Success for Sprint 1 is testing hypothesis H1, "will students publish their ideas?": five classmates publish on
the live app in under 5 minutes each and answer whether they would use it for their own idea.

## Positioning

Only people with a UPM email can publish, so every idea on the site comes from the UPM community. Anyone can read
without an account.

## Operating Context

- Visitors land directly on the published ideas; there is no separate landing page.
- Students publish through one short form of five fields.
- Demos happen on 6 Nov 2026 (experiment results, retrospective) in front of the Product Owner.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Browse: list of ideas showing title, pitch and date, newest first, without signing in. Empty state when there
  are no ideas. Clicking an idea opens its page with all five fields.
- Publish: signed-in UPM emails only. Fields are title (max 80), one-line pitch (max 140), problem (max 2000),
  solution (max 2000) and team members (max 500), all required. After publishing, the student lands on the
  idea's page. Ideas can't be edited or deleted yet.
- Accounts: anyone can sign up with an email and password; only `@alumnos.upm.es`, `@upm.es`, `@fi.upm.es` and
  `@dit.upm.es` can publish. Email confirmation is off for now; a 6-digit code is planned (IH-33).
- Later backlog, not built: public/private details, sector and stage filters, investor contact, comments, mentor
  ratings, new versions, drafts. No filters, categories, ratings or counts exist yet, so designs must not imply them.
- The structure of the forms, sign-up, sign-in and the ideas list must stay the same. A redesign changes how they
  look, not what they contain or how they flow.

## Brand Commitments

- The name is IdeaHub UPM.
- The site has a light and a dark version; it follows the system setting, and signed-in users can switch in the
  account menu.
- The current orange deck theme, fonts and lightbulb logo are not binding and may be replaced.
- The site must look professional and restrained: limited colour, nothing playful or decorative. The team rejected
  bright, multi-colour and zine-like directions as not serious enough for students and investors.

## Evidence on Hand

There are no testimonials, partners, investor names, usage numbers or press. Do not invent any. Sample ideas in
designs must be labelled as samples.

## Product Principles

- Publishing comes first: nothing stands between a student and getting an idea out in under 5 minutes.
- The ideas are the content: the students' words carry the page, not decoration.
- Open to read, UPM to publish: reading never needs an account, and the UPM rule is explained before it blocks anyone.
- Honest about what exists: never suggest features or traction the product doesn't have.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Usable by keyboard, readable at phone width without sideways scrolling, and clear in both light and dark themes.
